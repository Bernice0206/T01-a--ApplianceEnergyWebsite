Television Energy Tips

Screen size and technology can affect how much energy a television uses. Understanding these factors can help consumers make better choices when purchasing a TV.

How to Reduce TV Energy Use

Use Energy Saving Mode

Use the television's Energy Saver or Eco mode when available to reduce power consumption.

Adjust Brightness

Reducing screen brightness can help lower the amount of energy used by the television.

Check Energy Ratings

Energy ratings and annual energy consumption information can help consumers compare different television models.

TV Energy Insights: Data Story

This section presents findings from the cleaned Australian television dataset processed in T01(b). The dataset contains 4,759 available television models. The visualisations show patterns related to screen technology, screen size, power consumption and star rating.

Chart 1: What TV technologies are most common?

LCD (LED) was the most common screen technology in the dataset, with 3,794 models. LCD had 496 models, while OLED had 292 models.

Chart 2: What screen sizes are most frequent?

The 65.0-inch screen size was the most common in the dataset, with 777 television models.

Chart 3: Which screen type uses the least power?

LCD televisions had the lowest median power consumption at 71.3472 W. LCD (LED) had a median power consumption of 106 W, while OLED had a median power consumption of 112.7 W.

Chart 4: What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

The scatter plot shows a general positive relationship between screen size and power consumption. Larger televisions generally use more power, although there is some variation between models.

Chart 5: What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

The points are spread across different screen sizes, showing no clear consistent relationship between screen size and star rating in this dataset.

Chart 6: Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

The box plot shows differences in power consumption between television brands. The power consumption varies across brands, with some brands having models that use substantially more power. TCL had a model with power consumption reaching about 600 W.

Conclusion

The data shows that television energy consumption can vary depending on screen technology, screen size and brand. LCD had the lowest median power consumption among the screen technologies in the dataset. The 65.0-inch screen size was the most common, with 777 models. The scatter plot also shows a general relationship between larger screen sizes and higher power consumption, while the relationship between screen size and star rating is less clear.
